# Source evaluation rubric

Use these dimensions for high-stakes claims, conflicting sources, and sources whose authority is unclear. Assess each source for the claim at hand; do not calculate a universal domain score.

## Claim fit

- Does the source state or demonstrate the exact claim?
- Is the cited passage within the source's scope, population, jurisdiction, version, and time period?
- Is the answer making a stronger causal or universal claim than the evidence supports?

## Proximity

- Is this the original law, dataset, filing, specification, paper, statement, or direct observation?
- If it is secondary, does it identify and link to the original evidence?
- Are several apparent sources merely repeating the same upstream source?

## Authority

- Is the author or institution responsible for, qualified in, or directly involved with the subject?
- Is authorship identifiable, and is editorial or peer review visible where relevant?
- For software, use the documentation for the relevant version rather than an undated tutorial.

## Evidence quality

- Are methods, data, definitions, limitations, and uncertainty available?
- Can key assertions be checked independently?
- Does the evidence measure the claimed outcome rather than a convenient proxy?

## Currency and stability

- Could the fact have changed since publication?
- Is there a newer revision, correction, retraction, release, court decision, or official record?
- Does the page show both the event date and publication or update date when that distinction matters?

## Independence and incentives

- Does the source benefit financially, politically, or reputationally from the claim?
- Is sponsorship or affiliation disclosed?
- Can a self-interested claim be checked against independent evidence?

## Corroboration

Require stronger corroboration as consequence, novelty, controversy, or instability increases. Prefer sources with different evidence chains. Agreement among sites that copy one press release is not independent confirmation.

## Practical outcomes

Choose one outcome for each material claim:

- **Use:** strong claim fit and adequate authority.
- **Use with qualification:** useful evidence with a material limitation that must be stated.
- **Use only as a lead:** points toward evidence but should not support the final claim.
- **Exclude:** misleading, unverifiable, out of scope, superseded, or materially conflicted without corroboration.
